A timed treat dispenser drops a treat at a time you set, with nobody pressing anything. A button-press dispenser does the opposite: the dog triggers it. Both get lumped together under "automatic treat dispenser", and both are different products from the remote reward trainers and camera tossers covered in our [treat dispenser hub](/treat-dispensers). This guide looks only at scheduling and dog-triggered release, lists which documented models offer them, and is honest about the models that do not.

## Timed vs Button-Press: Two Different Jobs

The two categories solve opposite problems. A timed or scheduled dispenser is for routine: a mid-morning snack while you are at work, a treat spaced two hours after breakfast for a dog on a calorie budget, or a predictable "something happens" moment for a dog who is home alone for long stretches. Nobody marks a behavior, so it is not a training tool, and our hub's FAQ says the same. A button-press dispenser is enrichment and self-reward. The dog learns that pressing a pad releases a treat, which keeps a busy dog occupied and builds the kind of cause-and-effect confidence trainers use for puzzle work. It still is not a timer, and it does nothing while the dog ignores it.

Where the two overlap is the hardware underneath: a hopper, a release mechanism and a tray. The differences that matter are how the release is triggered, how far a treat travels (dropped at the base or flung across the room), and what size of treat the mechanism accepts. Those three points decide whether a listing fits your dog before any smart feature does.

## How to Choose a Timed Treat Dispenser

Start with the trigger, because manufacturers describe it very differently.

- **In-app schedule.** The device itself holds the schedule and fires whether or not your phone is on. Petcube documents this for Bites 2 Lite as "Auto Treats Scheduling" with a time, flinging power and serving count per entry.
- **Voice-assistant routine.** The schedule lives in a smart speaker. Furbo's Alexa page gives the example "tell Furbo to toss a treat every 30 minutes for 4 hours". That needs Alexa in the home, and it runs for a set span rather than at a fixed daily time.
- **Event-triggered toss.** Furbo's Calm My Pet plays a sound after repeated barking and can toss a treat once the barking stops. It is automatic, but it is not a timer, and Furbo lists it as a Furbo Nanny subscription feature.
- **No scheduling.** eufy's support page states that the Pet Camera D605 "cannot automatically dispense dog treats at random" and is "unable to dispense dog treats based on a preset schedule". That rules out a camera tosser this site already covers, even though it is a good fit on our [pet cameras with treat dispensers](/treat-dispensers/with-camera) page.

Then check the mechanics:

- **Treat size and shape.** Petcube says Bites 2 Lite takes "crunchy treats with dimensions of 1 inch or less". Instachew lists 2–12 mm for dry treats and up to 15 mm for hard or freeze-dried pieces. Soft or crumbly treats are not documented for either.
- **Drop or fling.** A tossing camera throws treats across a room; Petcube offers short, medium and long settings. A drop-style dispenser places the treat in a tray. Flinging is fun for a confident dog and startling for a nervous one.
- **Capacity in treats, not cups.** Petcube lists 1.5 lb of treats; Furbo lists about 100 treats; Instachew lists 1 L. None is a meal's worth of food, and that is by design.
- **Power and connectivity.** Both camera tossers here run on 2.4 GHz WiFi only, which matters if your router hides that band. The TRIXIE game runs on batteries or the included USB-C cable.
- **Subscription dependence.** Petcube says Bites 2 Lite works without Petcube Care, and its treat scheduling is an app setting. Furbo's video history and Calm My Pet sit behind Furbo Nanny.

## Scheduling Without a Dedicated Device

If your dog already eats from an app-controlled feeder, check whether its app can add a small extra "snack" meal of kibble at a set time. PETLIBRO's Granary WiFi page describes "meals or snacks" up to 10 times a day, and PetSafe's Smart Feed can "feed your pet a snack" through Alexa. That is kibble rather than a treat, and it comes out of the meal hopper, so it is portion-controlled by default. For dogs on a strict diet it is often the better version of a scheduled treat. ## Treats Still Count as Calories

A dispenser that fires three or four times a day can add a meaningful share of a small dog's intake without anyone noticing, because nobody hands the treat over. Before setting a schedule, weigh one day's worth of treats and compare it with the daily total from our [food calculator](/food-calculator). If the number is high, reduce the serving count, switch the schedule to kibble from a meal feeder as described above, or pick a lower-calorie treat that still fits the maker's size limit. Water matters too: a dog snacking on dry treats through the day should have a full bowl, and our [dog water dispenser guide](/dog-feeders/water) covers fountains and gravity options.

## Quick Checklist Before You Buy

- Decide whether you want a schedule (no dog input), a dog-pressed button (no schedule) or a remote you press yourself
- Confirm the schedule lives in the device or app, not only in a smart speaker, if you do not run Alexa
- Measure your treats against the maker's stated limit (1 inch for Petcube; 2–15 mm for Instachew)
- Check the WiFi band: both camera tossers documented here are 2.4 GHz only
- Read what sits behind a subscription before relying on it (Furbo's Calm My Pet and video history)
- Count the scheduled treats against the day's calories, especially for dogs under 20 lb

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a timed treat dispenser replace a meal feeder?

No. The documented capacities are 1 L, 1.5 lb of treats or about 100 pieces, and the mechanisms are built for treat-sized pieces. For scheduled meals use an automatic feeder; our dog feeder hub compares them.

### Does the Furbo 360° Dog Camera have a built-in treat timer?

Furbo documents scheduled tossing through Alexa voice commands, such as a treat every 30 minutes for 4 hours, and an automatic toss after barking stops through the Calm My Pet feature, which needs Furbo Nanny. We found no standalone in-app daily timer on Furbo's pages.

### Which treat dispensers let the dog press a button?

TRIXIE Memory Trainer 3.0 is the manufacturer-documented example: a separate release button the dog presses with nose or paw, working up to 40 m from the dispenser. A remote reward trainer such as PetSafe Teach and Treat is pressed by the owner, not the dog.

### Will a scheduled treat dispenser work for a cat?

Mechanically yes, as long as the treat fits the stated size range. Petcube lists Bites 2 Lite for pets generally, and our treat dispenser hub notes that scheduled dispensers are not dog-specific. Check the treat size and the fling distance for a smaller animal.
