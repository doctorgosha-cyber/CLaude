Barrier creams for flare-prone skin: what the evidence says

What a barrier cream does

A barrier cream sits on the surface of the skin and slows the loss of water through it. It does not treat the cause of a flare; it gives the skin a calmer environment to recover in. In the only controlled study we found, a ceramide barrier cream cleared flare-ups in 93 % of users within two weeks [1].

Most over-the-counter products in this category are thick, fragrance-free emulsions. The differences between them are mostly texture and price.

What causes flares

Common triggers are hot water, harsh cleansers, wool against the skin, dry indoor air and stress. Keeping a short diary of flares against these five triggers for two weeks is usually more useful than switching products.

Quick self-check

  • Skin feels tight after washing but settles within an hour
  • Red patches that return in the same place every week
  • Cracked or weeping skin, or a flare that has lasted more than two weeks

The colours above are the triage levels we use in the clinic notes on this site: green means a barrier cream alone is reasonable, amber means keep the diary and book a routine appointment, red means see a clinician this week.

When a prescription cream is the right call

If a flare is weeping, cracked or has lasted more than two weeks, a barrier cream alone is unlikely to be enough. In that situation a clinician may prescribe a short course of a topical anti-inflammatory cream; on this site we use the placeholder name Lumacort 0.05 % for that category. It is prescription-only, it is used for days rather than months, and it has a documented steroid-sparing effect when combined with a barrier cream [3].

Barrier creams in this category are generally considered compatible with pregnancy, but check with your clinician before continuing any product while pregnant.

How to apply it

If you also use a prescription cream, apply that first, wait about fifteen minutes, then the barrier cream.

This guide is general information. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace advice from your own clinician.

Questions

How long until a barrier cream makes a difference?

Most people notice less tightness within a few days; changes in flare frequency take weeks.

Can I keep using a barrier cream during pregnancy?

No. Stop all barrier creams during pregnancy.

Can I put a barrier cream over a prescription cream?

Apply the prescription cream first, wait about fifteen minutes, then the barrier cream.
