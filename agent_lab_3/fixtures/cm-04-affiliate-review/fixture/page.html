Pawline self-cleaning slicker brush review (six weeks with two cats)

By Tess Marlow · Published 18 August 2026 · Last updated 1 October 2026

★★★★☆

Verdict: a genuinely useful brush for long-haired cats that shed in clumps. The self-cleaning plate saves time, the pins are gentle enough for a nervous cat, and the handle fits a small hand. The price is high for what is essentially a slicker with a lever.

Vet-recommended and shown to reduce hairballs by 40 %, it is the brush we now reach for first.

How we tested

Two cats, six weeks, a brushing session every second evening. Mabel is a seven-year-old Maine Coon mix with a dense undercoat; Pip is a two-year-old domestic long-hair who tolerates grooming for about ninety seconds. We weighed the fur removed per session on a kitchen scale and noted how the cats behaved. We bought the brush ourselves, from this listing, in July.

Design and build

The head is a curved plate of fine stainless pins with rounded tips. Pressing the button on the back retracts the pins so the collected fur lifts off in one mat. The handle is rubberised and slightly shorter than a typical slicker, which suits a small hand and makes it easier to work around the belly.

In use

Mabel's sessions removed between 3 and 6 grams of undercoat for the first two weeks and settled at around 2 grams after that. The pins did not catch on her skin, and she started walking over to the brush when it came out of the drawer. Pip was less convinced; the brush is fine on her back but the plate is too wide for her legs.

The release button needs a firm press. With damp fur the mat sometimes stays attached to the pins and needs a second press.

Pros and cons

Pros

Cons

  • Expensive for a slicker
  • Plate too wide for legs and face

Alternatives

If your cat has a short coat, a rubber grooming mitt is gentler and a tenth of the price. For a dense double coat an undercoat rake does more in less time, though it is not as pleasant for the cat.

Questions readers ask

Does the self-cleaning button really work?

Yes for loose undercoat. Matted fur still needs to be pulled out by hand.

Is it suitable for short-haired cats?

It works, but a rubber mitt is gentler on a short coat.

Can it be used on dogs?

Yes. Our tester used it on a spaniel once a week without complaint.
